Assignment 1

Due March 2 · 13% of final grade

DesignStudents choose the graphs they think best suit each variable, then compare the overall trend with the trend within each group, seeing for themselves that a pattern visible within groups can blur when the groups are combined.

  • Choosing graphs
  • Central tendency & variability
  • Scatterplots by group
  • Effect sizes
  • Confidence intervals
  • z-test (NHST)

Instructions. Import Assignment1_data.csv into R and name it dat. This file contains anxiety scores of first-year undergraduate students across their first semester at York University. There should be three variables in your dataframe dat: Time (days), Anxiety and Program. There are three levels within Program: Psychology, Biology and Math.

Assignment1_data.csv138 students · 3 variables

  2. Generate two measures of central tendency and two measures of variability for each continuous variable.4 pts
  3. Graph and describe the relationship between Anxiety and Time (days).2 pts
  4. Graph and describe the relationship between Anxiety and Time (days) separately for each program (using a single plot).4 pts

Question 2 Answered by hand

It is known that the population mean anxiety score across all first-year undergraduate students is 10 (σ = 5), and that the minimally meaningful effect size (for the unstandardized mean difference) is 2 points. Compare the mean of participants in the Psychology program to the mean for all undergraduates (i.e., 10) using:

  1. An unstandardized mean difference and its interpretation.2 pts
  2. The mean of participants in the Psychology program and its 95% confidence interval (along with a statement on precision).
    Note: qnorm(.05/2, lower.tail = FALSE) = 1.96    2.5 pts
  3. A standardized mean difference and a statement regarding magnitude (small = .2–.5, medium = .5–.8, large = .8+).2 pts
  4. NHST, including your hypotheses and decisions (use α = .05). You can assume that the anxiety scores of first-year undergraduate students are normally distributed in the population.5 pts

Assignment 2

Due March 30 · 13% of final grade

DesignStudents analyze real data from a randomized controlled trial of online cognitive behavioural therapy for perfectionism among undergraduates. Like researchers, they isolate the treatment group, check whether the assumptions hold, and ask whether scores after therapy fall meaningfully below the clinical threshold, not merely whether the difference is statistically significant.

  • Real RCT data
  • Subsetting in R
  • Checking assumptions
  • Effect sizes
  • Confidence intervals
  • One-sample t-test
  • Correlation by hand
  • R Markdown

Question 1 Answered in R

Remember to knit an R Markdown file into either an HTML or PDF for Question 1.

Instructions. Import Assignment2_data.csv into R and name it perfectionism. This dataset originates from an RCT conducted by Chantal Arpin-Cribbie, which investigated the impact of online Cognitive Behavioural Therapy (CBT) on perfectionism in undergraduate students. (Students received a codebook for this dataset on eClass.)

Assignment2_data.csvAlso available in R: install the negligible package and run data(perfectionism)

The typical clinical threshold for socially-prescribed perfectionism (spp) is 60. We would like to investigate whether spp scores after CBT (i.e., scores under the variable mpshfpost.spp) are significantly less than the clinical cut-off. (Hint: subset the data to the CBT group first.)

Assuming α = .10, find:

  1. Whether the normality assumption is met and why.2 pts
  2. Use effect sizes to explore the magnitude of the effect. Be sure to interpret the magnitude of the effect sizes and include one or two sentences summarizing the effect size results. For Cohen’s d, you can use small = .2–.5, medium = .5–.8, large = .8+.4 pts
  3. The mean spp score after CBT and its 90% confidence interval (along with a statement on precision).2 pts
  4. NHST, including your hypotheses and decisions. You can assume that the socially-prescribed perfectionism scores of undergraduate students are normally distributed in the population.4 pts
